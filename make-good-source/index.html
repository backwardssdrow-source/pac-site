Before we begin

Commercial basics

Are the prices fixed?

They are starting investments. Final pricing reflects portfolio size, stakeholder groups, regions, languages, delivery mode, urgency, and the level of customization required.

Can the work be delivered remotely?

Yes. Every core offer is designed for remote-first delivery. Optional in-person work is separately scoped, and travel is not required for the core model.

Do you offer free pilots?

No. A limited number of founding-client engagements may receive preferred pricing in exchange for structured feedback and case-study participation, but the work is paid.

What happens after the fit conversation?

We confirm the business question, decision-maker, timeline, stakeholder scope, procurement needs, and whether the proposed service is the right starting point. A written proposal follows when there is a clear fit.

ERG Portfolio Diagnostic

The engagement

What this is

A structured review of how an ERG portfolio actually operates: governance, authority, sponsorship, funding, communications, measurement, and the labor required to keep the system moving. We combine document review, confidential stakeholder listening, and operating analysis to identify what is working, where responsibility is unclear, and which changes should come first.

Best for: Organizations with five or more ERGs, a new program owner, a reorganization, inconsistent sponsorship, visible leader fatigue, or a portfolio that has grown faster than its infrastructure.

Common signals

ERG leaders are carrying strategy, administration, events, communications, and escalation without clear authority.
Different ERGs receive materially different support, access, or funding.
Executives want clearer evidence of value but current measures mostly count activity.
A new owner has inherited the portfolio without a reliable map of decisions, risks, and obligations.

What changes

Outcomes

  • A shared picture of how the portfolio functions across groups and stakeholder roles.
  • Clear identification of governance gaps, participation burdens, and avoidable risk.
  • Prioritized recommendations tied to ownership, sequence, and practical effort.
  • A 90-day action roadmap that leadership can use immediately.

What is included

  • Kickoff and document request
  • Review of charters, budgets, planning materials, measurement, and communications
  • Eight to twelve confidential stakeholder interviews
  • Portfolio, governance, decision-right, sponsorship, and participation-labor maps
  • Risk and opportunity synthesis
  • Executive readout and 90-day action roadmap

Delivery

How the work unfolds

Week 1
Frame: confirm business questions, portfolio scope, stakeholders, and reporting boundaries.
Weeks 1–3
Listen: review materials and conduct confidential interviews across leaders, sponsors, and program owners.
Weeks 3–5
Map: connect roles, decisions, resources, burdens, and recurring breakdowns.
Week 6
Decide: deliver findings, priorities, ownership, and the 90-day roadmap.

What you receive

Deliverables

Current-State Portfolio Map
Governance and Decision-Rights Map
ERG Labor and Support Analysis
Risk and Opportunity Brief
Executive Readout
90-Day Action Roadmap

Working relationship

What we need from the client

  • Name an executive sponsor and day-to-day liaison.
  • Provide requested materials and access to a balanced stakeholder group.
  • Protect interview confidentiality and avoid seeking attributed comments.
  • Bring the people who can make decisions to the final readout.

Scope and pricing assumptions

  • Remote-first; one defined portfolio and primary operating region.
  • Starting scope assumes approximately five to fifteen ERGs and eight to twelve interviews.
  • Additional regions, languages, entities, or interview groups increase scope and investment.

Boundaries

What is not included

Implementation or ongoing administration
Event production or calendar ownership
Legal advice, privileged review, or employee investigations
A numerical ranking of ERGs or individual leaders

Questions

Frequently asked

Do we need a crisis to use this?

No. It is often most useful during a leadership transition, planning cycle, or before redesigning a system that has become difficult to sustain.

Will individual comments be attributed?

No. Findings are synthesized across patterns. Reporting and confidentiality boundaries are agreed before interviews begin.

Can the diagnostic lead directly into implementation?

Yes. The common next step is an Operating Model Build or a bounded Fractional ERG Office engagement.

ERG Operating Model Build

The engagement

What this is

A design and implementation engagement that turns ERG intentions into a usable operating system. We define who decides, who advises, how work moves, what support ERGs can expect, how sponsors participate, and how the portfolio plans, funds, escalates, communicates, and measures its work.

Best for: Organizations ready to replace informal or inconsistent ERG practices with a coherent portfolio model that leaders, sponsors, and internal partners can actually use.

Common signals

Charters exist, but decisions still depend on relationships or institutional memory.
Leaders and sponsors interpret their responsibilities differently.
Budget, intake, escalation, and annual planning vary substantially by group.
The organization has diagnosed the problem and now needs an implementable system.

What changes

Outcomes

  • A clear portfolio charter and role architecture.
  • Consistent decision rights and sponsor expectations.
  • Practical annual planning, funding, intake, escalation, and communication rhythms.
  • A measurement model that distinguishes participation, contribution, outcomes, and organizational responsibility.

What is included

  • Focused discovery and operating requirements
  • Portfolio charter and decision-rights framework
  • ERG leader, program owner, executive sponsor, and internal-partner role architecture
  • Budget, annual planning, intake, escalation, and communication processes
  • Measurement and reporting framework
  • Two working sessions with ERG leaders and sponsors
  • Implementation playbook and launch roadmap

Delivery

How the work unfolds

Weeks 1–2
Define: confirm operating requirements, constraints, and unresolved decisions.
Weeks 3–5
Design: build the core governance, role, and workflow architecture.
Weeks 6–8
Test: work through real scenarios with leaders, sponsors, and internal partners.
Weeks 9–12
Finalize and launch: deliver tools, facilitate adoption, and establish the first operating cycle.

  • Provide policy, budget, procurement, and communications constraints.
  • Assign internal owners who will operate the model after launch.

Scope and pricing assumptions

  • Pricing assumes a defined working group and two facilitated design sessions.
  • Global localization, multilingual materials, technology configuration, or extensive change management are separately scoped.

Permanent portfolio administration
Routine event production
Unlimited communications or change-management support

Do we need a diagnostic first?

Not always. A focused discovery phase is included, but a full diagnostic is recommended when the current state is contested or poorly documented.

Are the materials templates or custom?

The architecture is repeatable; the actual model is built around your organization’s size, risk, culture, and decision environment.

Who should be in the working group?

Usually the portfolio owner, representative ERG leaders, one or more sponsors, and relevant HR, Legal, Communications, Finance, or Employee Experience partners.

Presence Studio

Presence Scan

Understand what it is like to experience your leadership.

Duration4 weeks
Starting investmentFrom $12,500
Typical scope$12,500–$18,000
Common next stepSenior Presence Advisory or Presence Lab

The engagement

What this is

Presence is impact. The Presence Scan is a structured qualitative diagnostic that helps a leader understand the experience other people have in their orbit. It combines self-reflection, confidential stakeholder listening, contextual analysis, and a private debrief to identify strengths, friction, contradictions, and the few shifts most likely to improve leadership impact.

Best for: Senior leaders, executive sponsors, founders, and team leads whose effectiveness depends on trust, candor, clarity, and the experience they create around them.

Common signals

A leader receives polite or fragmented feedback but suspects the full experience is not reaching them.
A promotion, reorganization, or expanded span of influence requires greater intentionality.
People appear hesitant, overprepared, confused, or depleted after interactions.
Strong intent repeatedly produces friction around clarity, authority, boundaries, access, or follow-through.

What changes

Outcomes

  • A coherent view of how the leader is experienced across relationships and contexts.
  • Recognition of strengths that should be protected.
  • Clear language for recurring friction, mixed signals, and unintended impact.
  • Three to five practical shifts and a 60-day plan for testing them in real work.

What is included

  • Leader context intake and structured self-reflection
  • Agreement on stakeholder selection, confidentiality, and reporting boundaries
  • Six to ten confidential stakeholder interviews
  • Analysis across clarity, attention, authority, trust, consistency, boundaries, conflict, and repair
  • Private Presence Profile and Experience Pattern Map
  • Ninety-minute debrief
  • Written 60-day integration plan and follow-up

Delivery

How the work unfolds

Week 1
Orient: clarify context, stakes, stakeholder groups, and what the leader most needs to understand.
Weeks 1–2
Listen: conduct confidential interviews focused on concrete interactions and recurring patterns.
Week 3
Synthesize: connect themes without reducing the leader to a score or isolated comments.
Week 4
Integrate: deliver the private debrief, choose priority shifts, and build the 60-day plan.
Day 60
Review: assess what changed and whether continued advisory or team work is useful.

What you receive

Deliverables

Confidential Presence Profile
Experience Pattern Map
Strengths to Protect
Friction to Address
Three-to-Five Priority Shifts
60-Day Integration Plan

Working relationship

What we need from the client

  • Participate candidly in intake and self-reflection.
  • Help identify a balanced stakeholder group.
  • Do not pressure stakeholders to disclose what they shared.
  • Enter the debrief prepared to examine impact without treating feedback as a vote on personal worth.

Scope and pricing assumptions

  • Remote-first for one leader and one defined leadership context.
  • Starting scope includes six to ten interviews, one private profile, one debrief, and one follow-up.
  • Observation, multiple leaders, additional stakeholder groups, or board reporting require a larger scope.

Boundaries

What is not included

Clinical or psychological assessment
A validated 360 instrument or performance rating
Legal investigation or privileged fact-finding
Use as the sole basis for compensation, discipline, or termination

Questions

Frequently asked

How is this different from a 360?

A 360 usually aggregates ratings against competencies. The Presence Scan is a qualitative, contextual inquiry into the experience a leader creates and the practical choices available to them.

What does the sponsoring organization receive?

Reporting boundaries are agreed before work begins. A sponsor may receive completion confirmation or agreed commitments, but not raw notes or attributed comments.

What if the feedback is difficult?

The debrief separates identity from impact, examines context, and focuses on usable choices rather than praise or punishment.

Presence Lab

Turn intended values into a leadership experience people can actually feel.

Duration6–8 weeks
Starting investmentFrom $26,000
Typical scope$26,000–$45,000
Common next stepTeam integration or Senior Presence Advisory

The engagement

What this is

A facilitated team engagement that combines stakeholder listening, shared sensemaking, and repeated practice. The Lab helps a leadership team examine how power, clarity, attention, conflict, boundaries, and repair are experienced—then convert that insight into agreements and behaviors the team can use under real pressure.

Best for: Leadership teams, executive sponsor groups, and culture leaders navigating change, trust gaps, inconsistent behavior, or a gap between stated values and daily experience.

Common signals

The team has values but no shared practice for using them in decisions and conflict.
People experience different standards depending on which leader is involved.
Meetings produce ambiguity, guarded participation, or unresolved tension.
A new or reorganized team needs a deliberate relational operating model.

What changes

Outcomes

  • A shared language for the experience the team creates.
  • Greater consistency in how leaders communicate, decide, invite participation, and repair harm.
  • Team agreements tied to observable behavior.
  • A 90-day implementation plan that can be reviewed rather than merely announced.

What is included

  • Team context intake and stakeholder listening
  • Team Presence Pattern Map
  • Three facilitated practice labs
  • Work on power, consent, clarity, conflict, boundaries, trust, and repair
  • Team agreements and leadership commitments
  • Ninety-day implementation plan
  • Follow-up integration session

Delivery

How the work unfolds

Weeks 1–2
Discover: clarify context and listen for recurring experiences across the team and its stakeholders.
Weeks 3–5
Practice: facilitate three labs using real scenarios, reflection, and structured rehearsal.
Weeks 5–6
Commit: translate learning into specific team agreements and individual leadership commitments.
Weeks 7–8
Integrate: finalize the 90-day plan and hold a follow-up session where included.

What you receive

Deliverables

Team Presence Pattern Map
Three Facilitated Labs
Power and Participation Protocol
Conflict and Repair Protocol
Team Agreements
Leadership Commitments
90-Day Integration Plan

Working relationship

What we need from the client

  • Ensure the full leadership group can attend the core sessions.
  • Provide enough stakeholder access to ground the work in lived experience.
  • Bring real decisions and interactions into the labs.
  • Assign an internal owner for the 90-day integration plan.

Scope and pricing assumptions

  • Remote-first for one defined leadership team, generally up to twelve participants.
  • Starting scope includes stakeholder listening, three labs, agreements, and one follow-up.
  • Larger cohorts, multiple teams, in-person delivery, or extensive observation increase scope.

Boundaries

What is not included

Therapy or clinical group work
Privileged mediation or legal investigation
Disciplinary recommendations
A one-time motivational workshop without discovery and integration

Questions

Frequently asked

Is the Lab a workshop?

It includes facilitated sessions, but it is an engagement rather than a single event: discovery, practice, agreements, implementation, and follow-up.

Can this focus on executive sponsors?

Yes. Sponsor groups are a strong use case when inconsistent sponsorship is shaping ERG trust and effectiveness.

What happens if the team has active conflict?

We assess fit during discovery. Some conflict can be worked with; matters requiring formal mediation, investigation, or clinical support are outside scope.

What this is

An ongoing senior thought partnership for leaders making consequential decisions through relationships. The work creates a regular place to examine how situations are unfolding, prepare for high-stakes interactions, recognize recurring patterns, and choose how to use authority with greater clarity and consistency.

Best for: Executives, founders, culture leaders, and ERG sponsors who need rigorous reflection, decision preparation, and relational strategy without unlimited on-call coaching.

Common signals

The leader’s decisions travel through many relationships and create wide cultural effects.
High-stakes conversations require more than messaging preparation.
The leader understands a pattern intellectually but needs support changing it in practice.
A Presence Scan produced commitments that need sustained integration.

What changes

Outcomes

  • Greater recognition of recurring relational and leadership patterns.
  • More consistent alignment between intention, authority, and experienced impact.
  • A documented record of commitments, experiments, and learning over time.

Ongoing

Can the organization sponsor this?

Yes. The leader, sponsor, and advisor agree in advance on goals, confidentiality, and what—if anything—is reported beyond completion and agreed commitments.
